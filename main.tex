{\sffamily\bfseries\color{fusionink}EXECUTIVE SUMMARY}\par\smallskip
Fumo Lab is building \systemname as a \emph{compound intelligence system}
driven by two forms of progress: it becomes more capable when its underlying
models improve, and improves again as it learns how to understand tasks and
allocate those models better. Together, these forces create a new scaling
dimension---system capability that compounds faster than frontier-compute
consumption.

This opportunity emerges as intelligence demand and supply both change. Agents
turn isolated requests into trajectories where each transition can change the
capability, evidence, and judgment required next. Meanwhile, models are
becoming a heterogeneous portfolio of strengths, failure modes, and costs.

A router is a natural response, but request-level classification is not enough:
in agentic work, the load-bearing uncertainty may appear only after a tool
result, failed action, or testable candidate. Intelligence demand is partially
observable and must be discovered as the work unfolds.

Fusion therefore continuously allocates evidence-producing computation. It
maintains one task state, identifies the decision-relevant uncertainty, and
selects the model--role or tool best suited to resolve it. Multiple sources
contribute perspectives; one authoritative path preserves coherent answers and
actions. The result is a better performance--compute frontier across one-shot
problems and long-running workflows.

Each trajectory also reveals which uncertainties and model contributions
changed the outcome. These observations improve Fusion's capability map, task
understanding, and allocation policy, creating a bounded system-level recursive
improvement loop that remains evaluated, constrained, and reversible.
\end{tcolorbox}
