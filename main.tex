{\sffamily\bfseries\color{fusionink}EXECUTIVE SUMMARY}\par\smallskip
AI workloads are changing from isolated requests into long-running agentic
trajectories. Each observation and action changes the state of the work---and
with it, the capability, evidence, and judgment required next. Intelligence
demand is becoming state-dependent, multidimensional, and progressively
revealed through execution.

At the same time, intelligence supply is becoming more heterogeneous. Models
differ substantially in reasoning, domain knowledge, code, critique, tool use,
latency, reliability, and cost. The unit of work is becoming a trajectory; the
supply of intelligence is becoming a portfolio.

A model router is a natural response, but it solves a narrower problem. Routing
assigns a request after an upfront classification, often along a scalar notion
of difficulty. Agentic work cannot be fully classified in advance: its
load-bearing uncertainty may appear only after a tool result, a failed action,
or a candidate becomes testable. The required intelligence is partially
observable and path-dependent.

\systemname moves beyond request-level assignment to continuous allocation. It
maintains a canonical task state, identifies the decision-relevant uncertainty
in that state, acquires evidence from the model--role or tool computation best
suited to resolve it, and updates its allocation as the work unfolds. Multiple
sources can contribute perspectives, while one authoritative path preserves a
coherent answer and action history.

The result is a different way for AI systems to scale. Fusion improves when the
models available to it improve, and it improves again as it learns how to
allocate them. This interaction between supply-side model progress and
demand-side allocation progress creates the possibility of system capability
that compounds faster than frontier-compute consumption---across both one-shot
problems and long-running agentic workflows.
