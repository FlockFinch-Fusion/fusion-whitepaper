\section{Conclusion}

Agentic work has changed the demand for intelligence. A task is no longer one
request followed by one answer, but a trajectory in which the capability and
evidence required next are revealed through execution. At the same time, the
model ecosystem is becoming too heterogeneous to be consumed through a static
model choice. The next generation of AI systems will be defined not only by
the models they contain, but by how intelligently they allocate the
capabilities available to them.

Fusion is built for that transition. It organizes computation around the
uncertainty of the task, treats models and tools as conditional sources of
evidence, and preserves one state and one authoritative commitment path across
both answers and actions. This allows heterogeneous models to function as one
compound intelligence without turning their plurality into operational chaos.

The immediate result is the possibility of a Pareto improvement: frontier-level
performance with materially lower frontier-compute consumption, sustained
beyond one-shot questions into agentic workflows. The deeper result is a new
scaling dimension. Fusion advances as the supply of models improves and as its
understanding of tasks, capabilities, and allocation improves.

Model progress creates more intelligence. Allocation intelligence determines
how much of it becomes useful. Fumo Lab's ambition is to make those two forms
of progress compound.
