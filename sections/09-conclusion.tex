\section{Conclusion}

Language-model systems have more intelligence available to them than any single
model can express, but availability does not guarantee effective use.
\systemname introduces allocation intelligence as a complementary scaling
dimension: understand the task state, identify the evidence that is missing,
acquire it from the most suitable source, and stop when another call is worth
less than its cost.

The principle is implemented through one canonical state, multiple bounded and
non-authoritative perspectives, and one authoritative commitment path. This
architecture makes heterogeneous and asymmetric model pools coherent, converts
parallel outputs from votes into evidence, and extends naturally from static
questions to long-running agentic workflows.

\systemname improves when models improve---and can improve again by learning
how to use them. That second source of progress is the opportunity behind the
new scaling dimension: system capability that compounds faster than its
consumption of frontier compute.
