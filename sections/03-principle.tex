\section{Principle: Organize Computation Around Uncertainty}

The highest-level design principle of \systemname is simple:

\principle{Organize computation around uncertainty, not models.}

The principle changes the unit of orchestration. A conventional router treats
the unit as a model. An ensemble treats it as an answer. \systemname treats it
as an evidence-producing computation conditioned on the current task state.

\subsection{From entropy to decision value}

Language generation admits several notions of uncertainty. Token entropy
measures variation in the next-token distribution and can be high even when
many surface forms express the same meaning. Semantic entropy groups
meaning-equivalent generations and better captures answer-level uncertainty
\citep{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024semantic}. Neither is sufficient by itself
for orchestration. A system ultimately cares whether uncertainty can alter the
answer or the next action.

Let $S_t$ be the canonical task state, $Z_t$ a latent decision-relevant
variable, and $E_t$ the evidence already available. A candidate acquisition
$a=(m,r,v,b)$ chooses a model $m$, role $r$, context view $v$, and budget $b$.
It produces an observation $Y_a$. Its expected information gain is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{IG}(a) = H(Z_t\mid S_t,E_t)
  - \mathbb{E}_{Y_a}\!\left[H(Z_t\mid S_t,E_t,Y_a)\right].
\end{equation}
This is closely related to mutual-information acquisition objectives in active
learning \citep{houlsby2011bald}. But information gain alone can reward facts
that do not change the decision. The more relevant objective is the net value
of information:
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{VoI}(a) ={}&
 \mathbb{E}_{Y_a}\left[\max_d \mathbb{E}[U(d,Z_t)\mid E_t,Y_a]\right] \\
 &- \max_d \mathbb{E}[U(d,Z_t)\mid E_t]
 - C(a)-L(a),
\end{align}
where $C(a)$ and $L(a)$ represent monetary, token, and latency costs. Risk can
enter through the utility function or an explicit constraint.

\systemname does not claim to calculate a calibrated Bayesian posterior for
every open-ended task. The formulation supplies a design target. Current task
dimensions, evidence-readiness signals, model profiles, and stopping thresholds
are auditable approximations to this target; outcome data can make them more
empirical over time.

\subsection{Models as conditional sensors}

Under this view, a model is a conditional sensor. Its observation distribution
depends on the task, role, prompt, context, provider surface, and evidence
already present. Two sensors can be individually accurate yet jointly
redundant. A less accurate sensor can have high marginal value if its errors are
different and it targets a load-bearing uncertainty.

The capability of a model is therefore not a scalar leaderboard score. The
relevant quantity is closer to
\begin{equation}
 P(\text{useful evidence}\mid m,r,S_t,E_t,v,b),
\end{equation}
together with empirical error correlation and cost. This interaction view is
consistent with query--model routing research
\citep{ong2024routellm,song2025irtrouter}, but extends the target from selecting
one answering model to estimating the conditional contribution of another
piece of evidence.

\subsection{One state, many perspectives, one commit}

Plural evidence is useful only if it does not fragment authority. \systemname
therefore follows a second, architectural maxim:

\principle{One state, many perspectives, one commit.}

The system owns one canonical task state. Non-authoritative stages receive
purpose-built projections of that state and produce attributed evidence. They
do not mutate the ground truth and cannot directly create a client-visible
tool call. Exactly one authoritative path converts evidence into an answer or
state transition.

This yields four invariants:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item model agreement is not evidence of independence;
  \item a verifier result has authority only over the artifact and condition it
  actually checked;
  \item mutually incompatible plans cannot be spliced into one action; and
  \item further computation should stop when no available acquisition has
  positive expected decision value.
\end{enumerate}
