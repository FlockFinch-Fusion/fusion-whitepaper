\section{A New Scaling Dimension}

Fumo Lab's vision is an AI system whose capability is not fixed by any single
model and whose progress is not limited to the pace of model scaling.
\systemname is designed as a \emph{compound intelligence system}: it combines a
changing portfolio of models with an intelligence layer that learns how to
understand tasks, acquire evidence, and allocate capability over time.

The ambition is not simply to compose more models. Composition describes a
system's structure; compounding describes how its capability grows. Fusion
should become more capable when its underlying models advance, and become more
capable again when it learns how to use the entire portfolio better.

\subsection{Two coupled sources of progress}

We call progress in the models themselves \emph{supply-side intelligence
improvement}. Better training, data, algorithms, and compute expand the
capabilities available to the system. Fusion is designed to inherit these
advances without rebuilding its intelligence around a single permanent model.

We call progress in task understanding and model allocation \emph{demand-side
allocation intelligence}. A better allocation layer recognizes what a task
needs, what evidence is missing, which capability can supply it, and when
further computation is no longer valuable. It extracts more useful capability
from the model supply that already exists.

The two sources of progress are complementary:

\begin{equation}
  \text{system progress}
  \quad\longleftarrow\quad
  \underbrace{\text{model progress}}_{\text{supply}}
  \quad\times\quad
  \underbrace{\text{allocation progress}}_{\text{demand}}.
\end{equation}

The expression is not a proposed power law. It describes the architecture of
improvement. A stronger model expands the frontier of what Fusion can acquire.
A stronger allocator improves the conversion of that supply into outcomes.
Either can advance without waiting for the other, while progress in one changes
the opportunities available to the other.

\subsection{Capability that compounds faster than frontier compute}

The external manifestation of this vision is a better performance--compute
frontier. Fusion should know where frontier judgment is load-bearing, where a
specialist or efficient model can contribute more information, where an
environmental observation is required, and where the evidence is already
sufficient. System capability can then increase without multiplying frontier
compute proportionally.

This is not a vision of ``cheap AI.'' Cost is one of the signals that reveals
whether the system understands its own work. Redundant frontier calls indicate
that it cannot distinguish available evidence from missing evidence. Premature
use of a weaker source indicates that it cannot recognize where stronger
judgment is necessary. Allocation intelligence must improve both capability and
the productivity of compute.

\subsection{A bounded recursive improvement loop}

Allocation progress can itself become self-reinforcing. Every trajectory
reveals how Fusion interpreted the task, which computations it selected, what
evidence they produced, which evidence changed the commitment, and whether the
result succeeded. Those observations improve the system's task representation,
conditional capability map, allocation policy, and stopping behavior. Better
allocation then produces better outcomes and more informative future data.

This is recursive improvement in a bounded, system-level sense. The system uses
traces of its own behavior to improve how it assembles intelligence on the next
task. It does not imply unrestricted self-modification: the learning loop
remains versioned, evaluated, constrained, and reversible. Section~\ref{sec:learning}
describes the mechanism and its safeguards in detail.

\principle{Model progress expands the supply of intelligence. Fusion compounds
it through allocation intelligence.}
